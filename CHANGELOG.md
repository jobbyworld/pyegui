# Changelog

## Unreleased

- `segmented_control(..., tooltips=[...])`: a text shown when an option is hovered, one per option (None for none). Finder example: the modes Partielle and Complète of the Apparence window explain what they do in a tooltip
- Finder example: the look of the app is in one "Apparence" window, opened by the gear at the end of the theme control (bottom bar): the mode (Plat, Partielle, Complète, Image), the accent, the tabs, the blue folders (Windows) and, in the mode Image, a picture as the backdrop (a path, or the image selected in the files) with an automatic luminance, a saturation, a contrast and a veil for the theme chosen with a sun and a moon (which switches the theme of the whole app; only its sliders are shown), a blur, the opacity and the fit (`ctx.set_backdrop_image`); the changes show at once and are kept between runs. The settings menu is gone and the layout is always the unified one (the "Disposition unifiée" switch and the boxes layout are removed)
- `ctx.set_backdrop_image(source, luminance=1.0, saturation=1.0, blur=0.0, contrast=1.0, scrim=0.0, opacity=1.0, fit="cover")`: a picture as the backdrop of the window, drawn under everything (it shows where a transparent window, the layout rules or the glass style leave the window clear or translucent). The luminance and the saturation are a number or a pair `(light, dark)`, one value for each theme, followed when the theme changes, and the luminance can be `"auto"` (the picture is measured and brought to a brightness that suits the theme: lifted under the light theme, toned down under the dark one); `contrast` below 1 flattens the picture toward its mean brightness (the text reads better over the bright and dark parts); `scrim` is a veil over the picture (black under the dark theme, white under the light one, a number or a pair `(light, dark)`) that keeps the text readable; `blur` is a gaussian blur (done on a smaller copy, so it stays fast), `opacity` lets the backdrop effect show through, `fit` is "cover", "contain", "stretch" or "center". The file is read and scaled down by a thread (1920 pixels on its longest side) and the filters are applied again to that copy when a setting changes, so it can be called at every frame from sliders; `None` removes the picture; `ctx.backdrop_image_error()` tells why a file could not be read. GIF, WebP and BMP join PNG, JPEG and ICO. New example `examples/backdrop_image.py`
- `ImageView` and `image_view(view, source)`: an image viewer that fits the image to the room (never up), zooms with the wheel or a pinch around the pointer, moves with a drag (a hand cursor), fits again with a double click and zooms with Ctrl +, Ctrl - and Ctrl 0 (`zoom`, `fit`, `reset()`). `code_view(text, "rst")` colors reStructuredText: titles and their adornments, directives, comments and targets, list markers, fields and the inline markup. Finder example: images, reStructuredText and more source files (SQL, JSON, TOML, YAML, XML, HTML, CSS, C, Go, Java, text and logs...) open in the central view; the zoom controls of an image (zoom out, zoom in, fit) are a segmented control in its bar; Backspace and Escape in the viewer only leave it, and the file read is selected and has the keyboard again
- `set_cursor(source, hotspot=(0, 0), size=None)`: a custom mouse cursor for this frame, made from a `.cur` or `.ani` file (with its own hotspot), a `.ico` or `.png` picture, or an `.svg` (drawn sharp at the size of the cursors of the system). Call it at every frame that wants it, like the cursor of a widget; a frame that does not call it shows the usual cursor again. Windows only for now (the window answers `WM_SETCURSOR` itself, egui having a fixed set of cursors): elsewhere it returns False. The pan of the PDF viewer uses it: an open hand over the pages and a closed hand from the press to the release (`PointingHand` and the grab cursors of egui elsewhere)
- PDF viewer, pan: with `PdfView.tool = "hand"` a drag moves the pages and the point that was grabbed stays under the pointer from the press to the release (the offset is set from the press, not added up frame after frame, so nothing drifts), in both directions; the cursor is a hand (the pointing hand on Windows and Linux, the open and closed hand on macOS). `PdfView.scroll_offset` gives the scroll offset `(x, y)` of the last frame. Finder example and `pdf_viewer.py`: a "Texte / Pan" switch (a segmented control) in the tools of the PDF, to choose what a drag does
- Finder example, thumbnails of a PDF: a "Miniatures" button in the tools of the PDF shown in the central view replaces the pages with the thumbnails of all of them (`pdf_thumbnail` in a wrapping row, a slider for their size, only the ones on screen are drawn); a click on a thumbnail goes back to the pages, at that page, one at a time
- PDF viewer, one page at a time: with `PdfView.continuous = False` a wheel turn past the end of the page goes to the next page and past its top to the previous one (at its end), one turn per third of a second. Finder example: a "Continu" switch in the tools of the PDF shown in the central view
- `PdfView.replace_doc(doc)` shows another document in place of the one shown, at the same page and zoom (a file saved again, read again by a new `PdfDocument`). Finder example: a PDF opens in the central view like a markdown file (a double click or a click on its first page in Preview), with its tools (pages, zoom, fit, rotation, dark, search with the matches marked, copy of the selected text, password), and is read again at the same place when another program saves it; the Preview pane shows its first page and reads its information from the same document (one read of the file, by a thread)
- PDF viewer, search and selection: `PdfView.search(text, accents=False)` looks for the text in all the pages in a thread and marks every match (`matches`, `match_index`, `searching`, `next_match()`, `previous_match()`, `clear_search()`; the case and the accents are ignored by default, a line break matches a space). A drag in `pdf_view` selects text, a double click a word, a triple click a line, Ctrl+C (Cmd+C) copies, Escape clears (`selected_text`, `copy_selection()`, `clear_selection()`); `tool = "hand"` drags the pages instead. The links of the pages work (a click goes to the page or opens the address), `go_to(page, y)` goes to a place, `rotation` / `rotate()` turn the pages on the screen, `dark = True` draws them in light on dark, Ctrl +, Ctrl - and Ctrl 0 zoom. `PdfDocument`: `text(page)` reads the text of a page (it replaces pypdf's `extract_text`), `outline` lists the bookmarks, `render_png()` / `save_png()` draw a page as a picture, and `wait=False` reads a big file in a thread (`loading`; the views and thumbnails wait for it by themselves). `pdf_thumbnail(..., dark=True)`
- PDF viewer: `PdfDocument(path, password=None)` opens a PDF (`pages`, `page_size`, `info`, `needs_password`, `unlock`, `error`), `PdfView` + `pdf_view(view)` show its pages (continuous scroll or one page, `zoom`, `fit` to the width or the page, `page` to read or to go to a page, Ctrl + wheel zooms around the pointer, PageUp/PageDown/Home/End) and `pdf_thumbnail(doc, page)` draws one clickable page for a sidebar. The pages are drawn by threads with `craft-pdf` and kept as textures (memory budget, a coarser picture shows while a sharper one is drawn), the window never waits. New example `examples/pdf_viewer.py`
- `pdf_info(path)`: what a PDF says about itself (pages, version, title, author, subject, keywords, creator, producer, dates, encryption, `needs_password` and the six rights `can_print`, `can_copy`, `can_modify`, `can_annotate`, `can_fill_forms`, `can_assemble`), read in Rust with `craft-pdf` (the reader of the Craft libs, pinned at its rev with the `[patch.crates-io]` it asks for) and with the GIL released, so it replaces pypdf and its worker processes. Never raises: `error` tells why a file cannot be read. It is stricter than pypdf where pypdf is wrong (a date held by an indirect object, the `/Version` of the catalog over the header) and as tolerant where files are loose (a date without `D:`, read as local time when it has no offset). Behind the Cargo feature `pdf`, on by default. Finder example: `finder_pdf.py` calls it, pypdf is no longer needed (the `examples` dependency group is gone)
- `FileTreeView.items`: the paths of the rows shown at the last frame, in their order (the items of the open folders follow their folder, sort and filters applied), like the `items` of the list and icon views. Finder example: after Delete (Recycle Bin on Windows, Trash on macOS) the item that follows the deleted ones is selected
- Drag and drop with the system: files dropped on the window from the Finder (Explorer) come back in `dropped_files()`, and `hovered_files()` tells while they are over it. `start_file_drag(paths)` drags files out of the window to the Finder, the Explorer or any app (macOS and Windows; on Windows the call returns once the drag is over). On Windows the window has its own drop target: besides the files of the disk it takes the attachments dragged out of Outlook (new and classic) and other virtual files, written in a temporary folder and given by `dropped_files()`; `dragged_files()` gives the files of the last drag started by the window, to tell a drop on itself (a move) from a drop of another app (a copy). The three file views start that drag by themselves from a drag on an item (option `drag_out`, default True) and have a `drop_target` property: the folder under the pointer while files are dragged over the view (the pointer is read from the system, the window gets none), drawn framed. `system_modifiers()` gives the keys held now from the system (right while files are dragged, unlike `modifiers()`). `drop_operation(sources, folder)` says "move" or "copy" by the rules of the file managers (same volume: move, and Option on macOS / Ctrl elsewhere copies; two volumes: copy, and Cmd on macOS / Shift elsewhere moves), `same_volume(a, b)` tells if two paths are on one disk, and `FileTransfer` copies or moves files in a thread without freezing the window (`start(sources, folder, operation)`, then `running`, `progress`, `done`, `total`, `finished()` and `errors()` read at each frame; names that exist are not replaced: "name copy", "name copy 2"; a move between two volumes is a copy then the removal of the original). Finder example: the files dropped from the Finder or from a view, onto the folder shown or the folder under the pointer, go through these, with a progress bar and a message that says whether the drop moves or copies
- Fix: on Windows a view given `paths` (`FileListView.paths`, `FileIconView.paths`, `FileTreeView.paths`) found nothing when the capitals of the paths differed from the disk (the tags of the Finder keep lower case paths): the items of a tag were counted but not shown
- `set_blue_folders(True)`: on Windows the icons of the folders (`file_icon` and the file views) are drawn blue, in the style of the Finder, instead of the yellow of the Explorer; no effect on macOS and Linux. Finder example: a "Dossiers bleus" switch in the settings menu and an action (Affichage), kept between runs; the theme control (light, dark, system) moves from the sidebar to the bottom bar, between the count and the toggle of the docks
- Pinned columns: a right click on a title offers to pin the column at the left or at the right (menu in French: Épingler à gauche, Épingler à droite, Détacher); the other columns scroll horizontally (wheel, Shift + wheel or a thin bar). In `FileListView`, `FileIconView`, `FileTreeView` (the name is always pinned at the left of a tree) and `TableView`: properties `pinned_left` and `pinned_right` (by column key) to save and give back, option `pinnable`. The texts of the header menus and filters are in French
- `table(..., view=TableView(), types=[...])`: a table with an interactive header: sort (click), filter by the type of the column (text, number, date, choice), move, resize, and pin columns at the left or at the right (right click on a title; the others scroll horizontally). `selected` holds the index in `rows` whatever the sort and the filters. `TableView` keeps `sort_by`, `ascending`, `column_widths`, `column_order`, `column_filters`, `pinned_left`, `pinned_right`, and reads `visible_rows`. New example `examples/table_columns.py`
- `FileListView` and `FileTreeView`: the header of the columns is interactive. A drag on the border of a title resizes the column (a double click gives it back its width), a drag on a title moves it, a click sorts by it (a second click reverses the order), and the funnel at its right opens a filter that fits the type of the column: a text for a name, a minimum and a maximum for a size ("10 KB") or a number, a period for a date (Today, 7 days, 30 days, This year, or two dates), the values to keep for a kind. A filtered column shows its funnel filled. Options of `file_list_view` and `file_tree_view`: `resizable`, `reorderable`, `filterable`
- `extra_columns` accept a fourth item, `{"type": "text" | "number" | "date" | "choice", "value": function(path)}`: a column of your own then sorts and filters like the others. The values are kept for a second (`refresh_columns()`)
- `file_tree_view(..., columns=["modified", "size", "kind"], headers=[...], date_format=...)`: the tree can show the columns of the list at the right end of its rows; its siblings are sorted by the column chosen (`sort_by`, `ascending`) and the filters keep the folders, only the files are filtered. `header=False` hides the header
- New properties of the three views, to save and give back at the next run: `column_widths`, `column_order`, `column_filters` (text, `(min, max)` or list of values, by column key), `sort_by` and `ascending` (also on the tree; `sort_by` takes the title of a column of your own), and `clear_filters()`. `view.filter = text` is the filter of the column Name; giving the same text again at every frame (a search field) no longer erases the filter the user set in the header
- `FileListView` scrolls horizontally when its columns are wider than the view (the last columns used to be hidden); the header of `FileTreeView` is pinned at the top while the tree scrolls
- New example `examples/file_columns.py`; the Finder gives its extra columns types (Pages as a number, Last Opened as a date, Tags as a text, Notes and Prot. as choices) and keeps the widths, order and sort of the tree and the list between runs
- `text_edit_singleline(..., select_all=True)`: selects the whole text of the field at that frame, with `request_focus=True` a field opens on its value ready to be replaced or pasted over
- Finder example: a `…` at the end of the path bar turns it into a field holding the full path (Cmd+L too): Enter goes there, Escape cancels; Show / Hide hidden files (Cmd+Shift+.), hidden items drawn faded (and in italics in the tree) in the three views

## 0.6.1 (2026-11-14)

- CI: wheels for Windows on ARM64 (`win_arm64`), built natively on GitHub's `windows-11-arm` runner, next to Windows x64 and macOS
- Dock: the outlines of the tabs and panes are concentric with their fill (a thick outline no longer lets the fill show at the corners), a rounding larger than a tab is limited like egui limits the fill, and the title of a tab keeps clear of its corners and outline
- Dock: a top corner of a pane under any tab of its bar (not only the active one) stays square, so no notch of background shows under an inactive tab
- Layout rules: a corner of a box that lies in a corner of the window (gap 0) is rounded like the window, so its outline is no longer cut by the rounded window
- `Backdrop.LIQUID_GLASS` and `LIQUID_GLASS_CLEAR`: the glass is rounded like the corners of the window (read from macOS), no more square corners around the rounded content
- `ctx.set_backdrop_blur(radius)`: the blur of what is behind the window, from 0 (sharp) to about 64, macOS only (a CABackdropLayer behind the content, rounded like the window, the layer of the system glass views; looked up at run time)
- `ctx.set_glass_style(..., opacity=1.0, panels=None, windows=None, content=None)`: the transparency of the glass: `opacity` multiplies the opacity of every surface, the others set the opacity (0 to 1) of the panels, of the windows and menus, and of the sheet under the content in the partial mode; `sidebar`, `bars`, `central`, `dock` and `background` (the glass under the whole window, seen in the gaps between the boxes) give one level per part of the window (left/right panels, top/bottom panels, central area, panes of the docks: given, each pane and its tab are filled on its own, the tabs not active fainter and `DockPane(..., central=True)` takes the central level). `reset_style` followed by `set_glass_style` at each frame (a slider) no longer garbles the text: the fonts are installed once, at the end of the frame, and only when they change
- file views: `badges=f(path)` (colored dots, e.g. tags) on the three views, `extra_columns=[(title, width, f(path))]` on the list, `view.paths` to show items of any folders (a search, a tag), `keep_focus`, `scroll_to(path)`, `FileTreeView(activatable_dirs=True)`
- Terminal example: a Réglages pane sets the layout rules live (gap, rounding, opacity, inner margin, width and opacity of the outline, globally and for the sidebar, the top bar and the dock; the background between the boxes; the modern style (Inter, menus of glass); the blur of the backdrop) and copies the matching `layout=dict(...)`
- Finder example: the views are `FileTreeView`, `FileListView` and `FileIconView` with a `FileWatcher`, the title a `path_combo`, the path bar a `path_breadcrumb`; a dock at the bottom with a shell (following the folder shown) and a Python prompt; a slider for the transparency of the glass in the panes menu, and a Transparence tab to try every level of `set_glass_style` (theme, glass mode, global factor, sidebar, top and bottom bars, files, docks, windows, background), the box of the sidebar (gap to the content, margins left/top/bottom, rounding; all at 0 it fills the left of the window with square corners) and the blur of the backdrop live and copy the matching call
- file views: `FileListView` / `file_list_view` (columns Name, Date Modified, Size, Kind, sorted by a click on a header), `FileIconView` / `file_icon_view` (a grid of native icons) and `FileTreeView` / `file_tree_view` (folders read when opened). Selection with Cmd/Shift, rubber band, keyboard (arrows, Home/End, Enter, Cmd+A, Cmd+Up, type to select), history (`back`, `forward`, `up`), filter, hidden files, context menu. `examples/files.py` shows the three
- `path_breadcrumb(path)`: the folders of a path from the root with their icons, the ancestors shortened when room is missing and shown in full under the mouse; `path_combo(path)`: the name of a folder as a title, with its icon and a menu arrow under the mouse and the list of its ancestors on a click. Both take `on_right_click`, `on_middle_click` and `context_menu`, the breadcrumb also `on_click` and `on_double_click`
- `FileIconView.icon_size`: the size of the icons and of the grid, e.g. from a slider (the demo has one, like the Finder)
- `FileWatcher`: the folders the file views show are watched by the system (FSEvents, ReadDirectoryChangesW, inotify), a change made by any program shows at the next frame; `watch(path)` and `changed(path)` to reload a file an editor saved, `list_dir(folder)` for views of your own, `take_events()` for the raw events (the demo shows them in a Log dock)
- `Terminal(cwd=..., shell=..., args=..., env=...)` and `terminal(term)`: a real terminal (a shell in a pseudo terminal, colors, full screen programs, history, selection, copy and paste) to put in a `DockPane` or anywhere; `term.run(command)`, `term.send(text)`, `term.restart()`, `term.title`, `term.text()`. `examples/terminal.py` shows it in a dock
- layout rules: `run_native(..., layout=dict(gap=8, rounding=12, outline=..., sheet=..., opacity=..., inner_margin=..., background=..., sidebar=dict(...), dock=dict(...)))` or `ctx.set_layout(...)` give the spacing, corners, outline and opacity of every region (`sidebar`, `central`, `dock`, `topbar`, `bottombar`); a region or a call overrides the global rules. `sidebar_panel(f)`, `topbar_panel(f)` and `bottombar_panel(f)` draw a box with these rules, keep the same gap all around the boxes and clear the macOS window buttons; `Dock` and the central area follow the same rules: the tab of a pane has the sheet of the pane, the other tabs a fainter one
- `run_native(..., persist_id="app.name")`: the position and size of the window and what egui remembers (scroll, collapsed sections) come back at the next run
- `ctx.save_state(obj)` and `ctx.load_state()`: the choices of the user (theme, folder, view...) as JSON, saved with the window. `examples/finder.py` uses them
- `dock.save()` and `dock.load(layout, panes=None)`: the open panes, tabs, splits and sizes of a `Dock` as plain values, to keep with `ctx.save_state()`

- `scripts/make_shortcut_windows.py` and `scripts/make_app_macos.py`: launchers to start and pin an app (the Finder example by default) on the taskbar or the Dock
- `tree_view(..., context_menu=f)`: `f(ids)` is called on a right click on a node
- `tree.focus(focused=True)` and `tree.keep_focus`: give the keyboard focus to a `TreeView` (once, or at every frame unless a text field is being edited), or take it away, so an app can manage the focus of its zones
- `ctx.request_repaint(after=0.0)`: asks for another call of `update_func`, to run an animation
- `TreeNode(..., badges=[(r, g, b, a), ...])`: colored dots at the right end of the row of a node
- `DockPane(..., sheet=color)`: with layout rules, the background of the pane and of its tab, to give a dock its own color. The top corner of the pane under its tab is square
- `DockPane(..., hide_tab=True)`: hides the tab bar while the pane is alone in its tabs, for this pane only
- `pointer_pressed()`: True in the frame the primary mouse button went down, to know which zone was clicked
- docs: the channels of `RGB` (`color_edit_button_rgb`) are between 0 and 1, not 0 and 255
- `file_icon(path, size)`: also gives the icons of the Windows Explorer (via `IShellItemImageFactory`)

- Panels: `top_panel`, `bottom_panel`, `left_panel`, `right_panel`
- Windows and popups: `window`, `modal`, `tooltip`, `tooltip_ui`, `context_menu`
- Menus: `menu_bar`, `menu_button`, `menu_image_button`, `menu_image_text_button`, `close_menu`
- `grid` with `end_row`, `columns`, `frame`

### Data
- Tables: `table` for texts, `table_ui` to draw each cell, `column_widths=` to size the columns
- Tree views: `tree_view` with `TreeView` and `TreeNode`, multiple selection, keyboard navigation, drag and drop
  - `TreeView(default_open=False)` and `TreeView.is_open`: read the children of a directory only when it is opened
  - `TreeView(activatable_dirs=True)`: a double click on a directory activates it, the arrow still opens and closes it
  - `TreeNode(icon=uri)`: an image before the label
- Plots: `plot` with `PlotLine`, `PlotPoints`, `PlotBars` and `PlotRule`

### Dock
- `dock_area` with `Dock`, `DockPane` and `DockContainer`: panes that can be resized, rearranged by dragging their tabs and closed
- Styling with `Dock(..., **options)` and `Dock.set_style()`: tab bar, tab fills and text colors, outlines, spacing, gaps, drag preview, `glass=True` preset
- The tabs are rounded with the radius of the theme, the active tab has the background of its pane and a light outline joins them
- Widgets in the tab bars with `tab_bar_left`, `tab_bar_trailing` and `tab_bar_right`, given a `DockTabBar` with `add_pane`, `set_active` and `close`
- Pill tabs with `Dock(tab_style="pill")`: rounded tabs floating in the tab bar, the active one filled with the selection color
- `Dock.set_active` and `Dock.active_title`

### Markdown
- `markdown(text)`: headings, lists, tables, quotes, code blocks, links and images
- ```` ```mermaid ```` code blocks are drawn as diagrams: flowcharts, sequence, class, state, ER, pie, gantt and more. `mermaid_theme=` picks the theme, which follows the app by default

### Style and glass
- `ctx.set_style()` and `ctx.reset_style()`: colors, rounding, spacing, text sizes
- `ctx.set_glass_style()`: a translucent style looking like frosted glass
- Modern glass theme: `set_glass_style(accent=..., mode=...)`. In the partial mode, the default, the panels and menus are glass and the content an almost opaque sheet; the full mode makes everything glass. Titles and strong texts use Inter SemiBold. The macOS setting Reduce transparency makes the surfaces opaque
- `ctx.set_accent()`: the accent colors the selection, links, text cursor, active pill tabs and plot items without a color
- `run_native(fullsize_content=True)`: transparent title bar on macOS, the content goes under the window buttons
- Roomier menus with the modern theme, and a `menu_margin` option of `set_style`
- Native backdrops with `run_native(backdrop=...)` and `ctx.set_backdrop()`: Mica, Tabbed and Acrylic on Windows, Vibrancy and Liquid Glass on macOS. `Backdrop` holds their names, like `Backdrop.LIQUID_GLASS`

### Widgets
- `colored_label`, `selectable_label_clicked`, `radio_clicked`
- `switch` and `badge`
- `label(text, size=None)`; `label_response` and `selectable_label_response` return a `Response` with `clicked`, `double_clicked`, `secondary_clicked` and `hovered`
- `image()` and the image menu buttons take a `tint`, so one white SVG icon can follow the theme; `image_button_clicked(source, text, size, tint, selected, frame)`; `menu_button` takes a size
- `file_icon(path, size)`: the Finder icon of a file on macOS, `None` elsewhere
- `key_pressed("Cmd+ArrowUp", when_typing=False)`: keyboard shortcuts
- `RGBA` and `color_edit_button_rgba`: colors with transparency
- `drag_angle`
- Drag and drop of any Python object: `dnd_drag_source` and `dnd_drop_zone`
- `code_editor` takes `desired_width` and `desired_rows`

### Fixes
- The macOS wheels are built on macos-15, GitHub no longer runs macos-14
- The build failed on macOS and Linux: `log` and `pyo3-log` were Windows-only dependencies
- Liquid Glass and Vibrancy were drawn over the ui on macOS instead of behind it
- An app could freeze before showing its window when a text had a character missing from the default fonts, like an emoji: the Rust logs of other threads no longer go to Python logging, only their warnings to stderr
- The copy icon of the markdown code blocks was missing
